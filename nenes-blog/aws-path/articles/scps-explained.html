Service Control Policies: guardrails, not permissions

SCPs do not give anyone permission. They set the outer boundary that every identity in an account has to stay inside.

AWS Organizations in brief

Organizations lets you manage many AWS accounts together. A management account sits at the root, member accounts hold workloads, and organizational units (OUs) group accounts, for example Dev and Prod.

What an SCP does

A Service Control Policy sets the maximum permissions available in the accounts it is attached to. It can apply to an OU or a single account, and it is inherited down the tree.

An SCP never grants access by itself. An action works only if the SCP and the identity's IAM policy both allow it.

Example: protect logging

{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [{
    "Effect": "Deny",
    "Action": [
      "cloudtrail:StopLogging",
      "cloudtrail:DeleteTrail"
    ],
    "Resource": "*"
  }]
}

Attached to an OU, this stops everyone in its member accounts from switching off CloudTrail, even an administrator.

Things to remember

  • SCPs affect IAM users and roles in member accounts, including the member account's root user.
  • They do not apply to the management account.
  • Test on a small OU first, because a wrong deny can lock people out.

Where Control Tower fits

AWS Control Tower sets up a multi-account environment with sensible defaults and applies preventive and detective controls for you. Some of those controls are implemented with SCPs, so understanding SCPs explains what Control Tower is doing.

Check your understanding
  1. Can an SCP allow an action that IAM does not allow?
  2. Does an SCP restrict the management account?
  3. Why test an SCP on a small OU first?

Answer them in your own words, then compare with the linked AWS documentation.
